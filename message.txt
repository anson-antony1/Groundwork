## 0. TL;DR

- **What we built:** Groundwork tells a utility supervisor at **6 PM** which sites crews can reach **tomorrow**, which vehicles need **ground mats** (and roughly how many), when weather means **STOP WORK**, and what mats vs. postponing would cost. It uses real, timestamped USDA soil, Open-Meteo weather and NWS alert data for **300 real sites**, plus a tabletop **Arduino site beacon** that senses soil and shows the server's call.
- **Why it wins:** every other Sperry team will build the brief's checklist ("how dangerous is each site?"). We answer the question Sperry's actual business runs on: **"Can we get there tomorrow, with what, and when is it safe?"** Janet Sperry runs **Dakota Matting**, a temporary-access-road and ground-mat company. The mat decision is her world.
- **Prizes we're going for:** Sperry Tech PlanIT Control (primary) → Best Overall → Hardware track → MLH Gemini (and Tiger Data only if integrated for real).
- **The rule we never break:** weather STOP WORK always overrides access. Mats never make lightning or flooding safe. AI never sets a status.

### The AI-bias problem, and how we beat it

Every teammate's AI suggested the same Sperry project, and other teams use the same tools. **The AI-default Sperry project:** a map of power plants, a 0–10 score from a gradient-boosted model, a simulated lightning countdown, a Gemini-written briefing and Gemini photo tagging, named something like SiteWatch, CrewGuard or FieldShield. Build that and we're one of many.

**Our guardrails:**
- The story is about **access**, not a "safety dashboard."
- The **LLM is not the core.** Rules and models decide; Gemini only writes the briefing.
- Every number carries its **source and timestamp.**
- A **physical** demo nobody else has: real soil, a beacon that alarms, a judge who taps to acknowledge.
- A **real validation number** from a held-out year.

## 5. The product (what's actually built, Oct 17)

### Two separate decisions, by design

| Decision | Question | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Weather safety** (all vehicles) | How hazardous will tomorrow be? | 0–10 hazard score + `NORMAL / CAUTION / STOP_WORK / UNKNOWN` |
| **B. Ground access** (per vehicle) | Can a pickup vs. a heavy truck reach this site? | `GO / MATS / NO-GO` (all "for review") + mat count + cost comparison |

**Effective status:** STOP WORK wins over everything; missing or stale data becomes **supervisor review**, never "safe."

### Features vs. Sperry's brief

| Brief asks for | Groundwork |
|---|---|
| 50+ real sites | **300 real sites** (60 Florida + 240 across other states; OpenStreetMap substations) + the demo tray |
| Weather, heat, flooding | Open-Meteo forecast + recent rain, heat (apparent temperature), NWS flood and storm alerts |
| Soil saturation | **USDA NRCS soil survey** (drainage, clay) + 7-day and next-day rain → per-vehicle access call |
| Lightning: 10 mi, 30-min all-clear that resets | Crew lightning report stops every site within 10 mi; 30-min all-clear restarts on each new report; beacon shows `STOP - CLR 29:59`. **Labeled crew-reported, not a strike network** |
| Wildfire | NIFC active incidents; a fire within 10 mi raises CAUTION |
| Wildlife | iNaturalist alligator sightings within 10 km / 30 days → "brief the crew" note (labeled unverified) |
| Crew photo reports on a map | Photo + text + site + time → map pin, marked **unreviewed** until a supervisor reviews it |
| Ranked supervisor map | Operations map ranked by risk |
| 6 PM briefing | Deterministic facts → **Gemini** narration with a number check and a template fallback; **n8n** can email it at 6 PM |
| ML hazard index (0–10), historical training | **Learned STOP-WORK forecast** (advisory). Numbers below |
| (Our extra) | **Mats estimate + mats-vs-postpone cost**, **Hurricane Milton storm replay**, **physical site beacon**, MCP server for Claude Desktop, optional Auth0 roles |

### Numbers we can stand behind

- **Learned forecast** (logistic regression; 300 sites × 3 years = 328,800 site-days; inputs are Open-Meteo *archived forecasts*; labels are real NWS alerts from the Iowa Environmental Mesonet). Tested on **Jul 2025–Jun 2026, a year it never saw**:
  - **ROC AUC 0.85 vs 0.78** for our rule score (climatology 0.76). PR AUC 0.28 vs 0.20 at a 4.6% base rate.
  - Its riskiest 10% of site-days contain **52%** of STOP-WORK days (rules: 40%).
  - It also beats Anson's independent NOAA storm-report model on *that model's own target* (0.90 vs 0.89).
  - **Honest limit:** in Florida it only **ties** the rules (0.80 vs 0.81), because summer storms happen almost every afternoon. That's why it stays **advisory** and never sets a status.
- **Data coverage:** weather for 300/300 sites, NWS checked 300/300, USDA soil for 248/300 (the rest show UNKNOWN, by design).
- **Hurricane Milton replay** (Oct 9, 2024): real archived weather and the warnings active that day; **all 60 Florida sites reach STOP WORK**, while Oregon doesn't, so a Florida storm never stops work out west.
- **Mats example:** same damp ground, the pickup is GO while the heavy truck needs roughly **42–48 mats** (the number in the script). ⚠️ An earlier API check showed 13–14 mats with default route settings. **Read the real number off the demo site before judging** and say that one. Costs are editable planning assumptions (example: mats $900 vs postpone $2,800).
- **Tests:** 204 backend + hardware tests pass; frontend typecheck, lint, 159 tests and build pass.
- **Beacon safety:** unplug USB and within 6 s the beacon shows `NO LINK` and never shows green on stale data.

### What AI does, and doesn't

- **Does:** Gemini writes the 6 PM summary from structured facts (with a number check and a non-AI fallback); a small ML model gives an advisory forecast.
- **Doesn't:** set STOP WORK, decide access, certify ground strength, or tag photos. **AI photo tagging is not implemented. Don't claim it.**

### The site beacon (hardware)

An Arduino Uno box beside a soil tray. It **senses** (Grove moisture, light, barometer/temp), **shows the server's decision** (Grove RGB LCD, red/green LEDs, buzzer) and **takes crew input** (touch = acknowledge alarm, long button press = flag a site). It talks to the laptop over USB serial (115200 baud) through `hardware/serial_bridge.py`. **The Arduino never computes safety status.**

| Status | LCD | LEDs | Buzzer |
|---|---|---|---|
| GO | green | green | off |
| MATS | amber | both | off |
| NO-GO | red | red | off |
| STOP | red | red blinking | pulses until touched (touch acknowledges; doesn't clear) |
| UNKNOWN / stale (6 s) | white | alternating | off |

## 8. Judge Q&A crib (short answers)

- **"Does it guarantee trucks won't get stuck?"** No. It's advance planning from soil surveys, forecasts and configurable vehicle rules; a supervisor still assesses the site.
- **"Isn't your ML just rules?"** The safety engine is transparent rules on purpose. Next to it is a real model trained on 3 years at 300 sites and tested on a year it never saw: AUC 0.85 vs 0.78. In Florida it only ties, so it stays advisory.
- **"Can mats override lightning?"** Never.
- **"Does touching the beacon clear STOP WORK?"** No. It logs the acknowledgment; only the weather logic or the all-clear timer clears it.
- **"Why Arduino, not a Pi?"** The Uno has analog inputs for moisture and light (a Pi doesn't), and USB keeps the demo independent of venue Wi-Fi.
- **"What if the beacon disconnects?"** `NO LINK` within 6 s; it never shows green on stale data.
- **"Perry Weather already does this."** It does lightning and heat at fixed sites with its own hardware. We'd integrate it. Our contribution is the per-vehicle access and mats call for tomorrow.
- **"How is this different from Common Ground?"** Different question and timescale: overlapping projects years ahead vs. tonight's access call for tomorrow. Same discipline: a source on every number.
- **"Who pays?"** Utilities' operations teams (fewer stuck trucks, fewer wasted mat hauls and idle crews) and mat vendors (demand forecast for overnight staging).
- **"How would you deploy and secure it?"** FastAPI + SQLite today (Tiger Data/Postgres for telemetry), Auth0 roles with backend token checks, token-protected sensor writes, secrets out of the repo, offline snapshot so the plan still works when an API is down.
- **"The brief says power plants. Why substations?"** Substations are where utility crews actually go day to day, and OpenStreetMap gives real coordinates for 300 of them nationwide. The site list is just a file: EIA-860M plants, or a utility's own job sites, drop in the same way. *(Even better, if someone has 30 minutes: add a batch of EIA-860M Florida plants so we meet the brief word for word.)*
- **"Why not real satellite lightning?"** NOAA's GOES lightning data is free and would plug into the same 10-mile / 30-minute rule. We chose crew reports for the demo so the rule fires on cue and is honestly labeled.
- **"How do you know the data is real?"** Every site shows provider, fetch time and missing fields; we count coverage (USDA 248/300) instead of hiding gaps.

## 9. Devpost submission draft (edit and paste)

**Name:** Groundwork
**Tagline:** Tonight's plan for tomorrow's crews: who can reach which site, which trucks need mats, and when it's not safe to work.

**Inspiration:** The night before a job, a utility supervisor juggles weather apps, soil maps, spreadsheets and texts from crews to answer a simple question: can our trucks get in tomorrow, and is it safe to work? Getting it wrong means a heavy truck stuck at a remote site, or mats and crews paid for nothing. Sperry Tech's PlanIT Control challenge asked for a hazard platform; we focused on the access decision underneath it.

**What it does:** Groundwork ranks 300 real sites every evening. For each one it scores tomorrow's weather hazard (rain, gusts, heat, NWS alerts; lightning, flood and storm warnings force STOP WORK), makes a per-vehicle access call from USDA soil data and recent rain (GO / MATS / NO-GO), estimates mats and compares mats vs. postponing, takes crew photo and lightning reports (a 30-minute all-clear that resets with every strike), and writes a 6 PM briefing. A learned forecast, tested on a held-out year, gives an advisory chance of a STOP-WORK day. A tabletop Arduino beacon reads real soil and shows the server's decision with an LCD, lights and an alarm a crew member must acknowledge.

**How we built it:** FastAPI + SQLite backend with deterministic hazard and access rules; React/TypeScript/Vite frontend with Leaflet maps; Open-Meteo, USDA NRCS, NWS, NIFC, iNaturalist and Iowa Environmental Mesonet data with timestamps on everything; a logistic-regression forecast trained on 328,800 site-days of archived forecasts and real NWS alerts; Gemini for the briefing narrative with a number check and a template fallback; n8n for the 6 PM email; an Arduino Uno with Grove sensors and a Python serial bridge; Auth0 roles; an MCP server so Claude Desktop can query the API.

**Challenges:** keeping safety and access as two separate decisions that can't be averaged together; making missing data read as "review," never "safe"; getting a cheap soil probe to land reliably in the MATS band; wiring Grove modules to an Uno without a base shield; keeping the demo alive offline.

**Accomplishments:** 300 real sites with provenance; a forecast that beats our own rules on a year it never saw (AUC 0.85 vs 0.78) and an honest note where it doesn't; a physical loop from soil to server to alarm in about 2 seconds; 360+ automated tests.

**What we learned:** honest labels are a feature; the hardest part of safety software is what it refuses to say.

**What's next:** real job sites and access routes uploaded by a utility, mat counts along the actual route (OpenStreetMap path split by soil unit), ground-pressure classes per vehicle, licensed lightning feeds, and crews' "got stuck / mats were fine" logs tuning the thresholds.

**Built with:** python, fastapi, sqlite, react, typescript, vite, leaflet, scikit-learn, gemini, n8n, auth0, arduino, grove, open-meteo, usda-nrcs, nws, mcp

## 10. Hardware cheat sheet (Anson)

- **Pin map:** moisture **A0** · light **A2** · touch **D2** · button **D4** · buzzer **D6** · red LED **D8** · green LED **D9** · LCD on **A4/A5** · barometer on **SDA/SCL** (top-left; same bus as A4/A5) · 5V/GND to the breadboard strips.
- **Grove cables have a white plug on both ends.** Either use a Grove Base Shield (check the kit box or the MLH desk), or push male-to-male jumpers into the free plug's yellow/red/black holes (plus white for the LCD and barometer). 29 jumpers total.
- **LED resistors:** use the 5-band **red-red-black-black-brown (220 Ω)** and **red-red-black-black-red (220 Ω)**. Skip the tan ~7.5 kΩ one.
- **Demo tray:** dry corner (GO), **damp patch reading 40–60** (MATS; the beacon always shows the heavy truck), wet spot (NO-GO). Pouring straight on the probe skips MATS, so MATS comes from the damp patch.
- **Fallback:** if USB fails, run the bridge in `--replay` mode with the recorded tray CSV (labeled "Recorded demo").
- **Visual wiring guide:** the "Groundwork Beacon Wiring" page (10 illustrated steps).
